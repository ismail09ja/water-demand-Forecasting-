OBSERVATIONS

1. Seasonal Patterns

The dataset shows strong seasonal variations in water demand:
- Higher demand during summer months (June-August) due to increased temperature
- Lower demand during winter months (December-February) with cooler temperatures
- Seasonal patterns follow a sinusoidal pattern with peak demand in mid-summer
- Temperature shows a strong positive correlation with water demand

2. Weekly Cycle Patterns

From the time series analysis:
- Water demand is higher on weekends (Saturday-Sunday) compared to weekdays
- Weekly patterns show consistent cyclical behavior throughout the year
- The day_of_week feature is a strong predictor, with weekend factor increasing demand by approximately 10%

3. Weather Impact Findings

From the correlation and relationship analysis:
- Temperature shows a strong positive correlation with water demand - hotter days lead to significantly higher consumption
- Rainfall shows a negative correlation with water demand - more rainfall reduces outdoor water usage
- Temperature effect: approximately 2% increase in demand per 10°C temperature rise above baseline
- Rainfall effect: up to 5% reduction in demand during heavy rainfall periods

4. Population Growth Trend

The population_index feature shows:
- Gradual upward trend with 2% annual growth rate
- Population growth contributes to long-term increasing water demand
- This trend is captured well by the models, enabling accurate long-term forecasting

5. Model Performance Observations

From the evaluation results:
- Quantile Regression significantly outperforms SARIMAX (R²: 0.84 vs -0.54)
- Quantile Regression achieves excellent accuracy with MAPE of only 3.78%
- SARIMAX struggles with the complex feature relationships and shows poor fit
- The machine learning approach (Quantile Regression) captures non-linear relationships better than traditional time series methods

6. Uncertainty Quantification Patterns

From the quantile forecast analysis:
- 80% prediction interval coverage: 76.78% (close to target, indicating well-calibrated uncertainty)
- Lower quantile (10%) predictions help identify low-demand scenarios
- Upper quantile (90%) predictions help identify high-demand risk periods
- Uncertainty intervals widen during extreme weather conditions, appropriately reflecting increased forecast uncertainty

7. Residual Analysis Findings

From the residual plots:
- Quantile Regression residuals show relatively random distribution with no clear patterns
- Residuals are centered around zero, indicating unbiased predictions
- No strong heteroscedasticity observed, suggesting consistent model performance across different demand levels
- SARIMAX residuals show systematic patterns, indicating model misspecification

8. Lag Feature Importance

From feature analysis:
- demand_lag_7 (7-day lag) is highly predictive, capturing weekly patterns
- demand_lag_1 (1-day lag) captures short-term dependencies
- demand_lag_30 (30-day lag) helps capture monthly/seasonal patterns
- Rolling statistics (7-day and 30-day means) provide smoothed trend information

9. Time Series Characteristics

From the data overview visualizations:
- Water demand shows clear trend component (upward due to population growth)
- Strong seasonal component with annual cycles
- Weekly cyclical patterns are consistent throughout the dataset
- Some random noise present, but overall patterns are well-defined

10. Forecast Uncertainty Patterns

From the uncertainty interval visualizations:
- Prediction intervals are narrower during stable weather periods
- Intervals widen appropriately during seasonal transitions (spring/fall)
- Extreme weather events (heatwaves, heavy rainfall) show wider uncertainty bands
- The model correctly identifies periods of higher forecast uncertainty

11. Feature Relationship Patterns

Key relationships observed:
- Higher temperatures consistently lead to increased water demand
- Rainfall events cause temporary reductions in water consumption
- Population growth creates a gradual upward trend in baseline demand
- Seasonal factors compound with weather effects (summer heat + high demand)
- Weekly patterns remain consistent regardless of season or weather

12. Model Comparison Insights

From comparing SARIMAX and Quantile Regression:
- SARIMAX fails to capture complex feature interactions (negative R²)
- Quantile Regression excels at learning non-linear relationships between features
- Machine learning approach better handles multiple exogenous variables
- Quantile Regression provides additional value through uncertainty quantification
- The ensemble of features (weather + time + lags) works better with ML models than pure time series models
